# Forecasting Mainland China Inflation

**DS607 Statistics for Data Science individual course project**

This project evaluates whether parsimonious seasonal time-series models improve out-of-sample forecasts of mainland China's headline and core consumer price inflation relative to a simple operational benchmark. It also examines whether explicit Chinese New Year information adds explanatory value to the headline month-on-month inflation model.

The project was completed individually with instructor approval as part of the Singapore Management University Master of Data Science and Engineering programme.

## Research questions

The project addresses two related but distinct questions.

### Forecasting question

Do parsimonious seasonal time-series models produce practically meaningful improvements in out-of-sample forecasts of mainland China's headline and core CPI inflation relative to a five-year calendar-month operational benchmark?

### Inference question

Holding fixed Gregorian month effects and a matched SARIMA error specification constant, do Chinese New Year pressure and post-CNY normalisation regressors jointly improve the maximised likelihood of the headline month-on-month inflation model?

The second question is evaluated using a likelihood-ratio test. It concerns explanatory fit and is distinct from the out-of-sample forecast comparison.

## Data

### CPI series

The underlying CPI statistics originate from the National Bureau of Statistics of China and were accessed through CEIC Data via the Singapore Management University Libraries subscription.

The analysis uses four monthly mainland China CPI series:

- published headline month-on-month inflation;
- published headline year-on-year inflation;
- published core month-on-month inflation;
- published core year-on-year inflation.

The common headline-and-core modelling sample begins in January 2006. The local extract used for the final analysis ends in August 2026.

Published month-on-month inflation is modelled directly. Forecast monthly rates are compounded into reconstructed CPI index paths and converted into year-on-year inflation forecasts for comparison with the corresponding published year-on-year series.

### Chinese New Year calendar

Publicly documented Chinese New Year dates are used to construct two deterministic moving-holiday regressors:

- `cny_pressure`, representing the period from 14 days before through 14 days after Chinese New Year;
- `cny_normalisation`, representing the period from 15 through 28 days after Chinese New Year.

Each phase is allocated fractionally across the Gregorian months containing its affected days.

## Analytical design

### 1. Data audit and CPI reconstruction

Published month-on-month inflation rates are compounded into normalised CPI index paths. Reconstructed year-on-year inflation is compared with the published year-on-year series to audit the transformation and quantify discrepancies that may arise from rounding.

### 2. Operational benchmark

The benchmark forecasts each calendar month's inflation using the preceding five values for the same calendar month. Forecasts beyond 12 months are generated recursively, so no future realised inflation rate enters a historical forecast.

### 3. Model development

The model-development sample covers January 2006 through December 2014. Candidate SARIMA and SARIMAX specifications are assessed using information criteria, residual diagnostics, root validity, fitting reliability, and expanding-window internal validation.

Model specifications are locked before the final evaluation period is used.

### 4. Final forecast evaluation

The final evaluation uses expanding-window forecast origins from December 2014 through August 2024, with horizons of up to 24 months and realised targets through August 2026.

There are 117 attempted forecast origins. Accuracy comparisons use 104 common successful origins for each target so that every model within a comparison is evaluated on the same origin-horizon observations. Reliability is reported separately across all attempted origins.

### 5. Chinese New Year inference

A matched likelihood-ratio test compares restricted and unrestricted headline month-on-month inflation models over the January 2006 to December 2014 development sample.

The unrestricted model adds the two deterministic Chinese New Year regressors while holding the Gregorian month indicators and SARIMA error specification fixed. The test evaluates explanatory fit. It does not, by itself, establish improved out-of-sample forecasting performance.

## Final model ladder

### Headline inflation

- **Operational benchmark:** five-year recursive same-calendar-month average.
- **Conventional challenger:** CNY-ignorant SARIMA with order `(1, 0, 1)` and seasonal order `(1, 0, 1, 12)`.
- **Primary CNY-aware challenger:** seasonal-MA SARIMAX with order `(1, 0, 1)`, seasonal order `(0, 0, 1, 12)`, Gregorian month indicators, `cny_pressure`, and `cny_normalisation`.
- **CNY-aware robustness model:** seasonal-AR SARIMAX with order `(1, 0, 1)`, seasonal order `(1, 0, 0, 12)`, Gregorian month indicators, `cny_pressure`, and `cny_normalisation`.

### Core inflation

- **Operational benchmark:** five-year recursive same-calendar-month average.
- **Primary statistical model:** ARMA(1,1) seasonal ARMA with order `(1, 0, 1)` and seasonal order `(1, 0, 1, 12)`.
- **Reliability robustness model:** MA(2) seasonal ARMA with order `(0, 0, 2)` and seasonal order `(1, 0, 1, 12)`.

## Main findings

### Headline forecasting

The operational benchmark is the strongest general performer beyond horizon 1 and has perfect operational reliability. The CNY-aware seasonal-AR model produces the best horizon-1 RMSE and complete convergence, but neither CNY-aware model delivers superior medium- or long-horizon year-on-year forecasts. The CNY-aware seasonal-MA model also retains a near-invertible seasonal MA root.

### Core forecasting

The operational benchmark leads at short horizons. The locked ARMA(1,1) seasonal ARMA model performs best at the displayed medium and long horizons, although both statistical core models experience some fitting failures.

### Forecast bias and reliability

Negative mean errors indicate systematic headline overforecasting, with the bias becoming larger at longer horizons. Core forecast bias is smaller. Accuracy and reliability are therefore reported separately rather than treating a model's loss measure as its only operational property.

### Chinese New Year inference

The matched likelihood-ratio test gives:

- likelihood-ratio statistic: `28.9853`;
- degrees of freedom: `2`;
- p-value: `5.081e-07`.

The null hypothesis is rejected. Conditional on the fixed Gregorian month effects and matched SARIMA error specification, the two CNY regressors jointly improve the maximised likelihood of the headline month-on-month inflation model.

The unrestricted estimates are:

- CNY pressure: `+1.2106` percentage points, with 95% confidence interval `[0.2766, 2.1445]`;
- post-CNY normalisation: `-0.6105` percentage points, with 95% confidence interval `[-1.0394, -0.1816]`.

These estimates support the expected pressure-and-normalisation pattern. They do not imply that the CNY-aware model must forecast better at every horizon. The likelihood-ratio result and the forecast evaluation answer different questions.

## Interpretation caution

The project distinguishes three forms of evidence:

1. **Information criteria and residual diagnostics** describe fitted-model adequacy during development.
2. **The likelihood-ratio test** assesses whether the two CNY regressors jointly improve maximised in-sample likelihood under a matched specification.
3. **Expanding-window forecast errors** assess out-of-sample predictive performance.

Evidence from one category is not treated as proof of a conclusion belonging to another category.

## Limitations

- The operational benchmark and statistical models use published inflation releases rather than real-time vintages.
- The reconstructed CPI indexes are analytical transformations, not official published index-level series.
- Published month-on-month rates may be rounded, creating differences between published and reconstructed year-on-year inflation.
- The Chinese New Year regressors use deterministic calendar windows and do not model every possible holiday-related transmission mechanism.
- Some statistical specifications exhibit fitting failures or roots near the stationarity or invertibility boundaries.
- Forecast comparisons therefore report both accuracy on common successful origins and reliability across all attempted origins.
